# RADSAFE — Project Scope

**Version:** 2.0 | **Date:** 27 November 2026

## 1. Vision

RADSAFE (Patient-Centric Longitudinal CT Radiation Dose Tracking & Cumulative Exposure Analysis System) is an academic web prototype that organizes CT radiation-dose information from supported DICOM RDSR files into traceable patient examination histories and scientifically qualified summaries.

**Core concept:** one internal RADSAFE Patient ID can be associated with multiple CT examinations over time, with safe patient matching and retained source provenance.

## 2. Problem statement

CT examinations produce dose-related information that may be distributed across separate RDSR files and visits. Reviewing a patient's longitudinal CT history can therefore require manual collection, interpretation and calculation. RADSAFE aims to centralize supported records, preserve event-level information and make valid summaries easier for authorized staff and Medical Physicists to review.

## 3. Objectives

1. Accept supported CT RDSR files through manual upload.
2. Validate DICOM files and identify supported CT RDSR content.
3. Extract available patient, examination, scanner, protocol, irradiation-event and dose information.
4. Maintain a patient-centred longitudinal examination history.
5. Preserve CTDIvol and DLP at the appropriate source/event level with units and provenance.
6. Calculate estimated Effective Dose only when all required inputs and a validated applicable coefficient are available.
7. Compare compatible examination groups with sourced DRL values using appropriate metrics and statistics.
8. Generate three MVP report types: Examination Dose, Patient Dose Summary and Dose Analytics & DRL.
9. Preserve traceability for source files, parser outcomes, calculations, references and reports.

## 4. Intended users

- **Administrator:** manages institutional setup and authorized staff accounts.
- **Medical Physicist:** reviews RDSR data, event-level dose indicators, eligible estimates, DRL comparisons and professional reports.
- **Authorized Staff:** uploads permitted files, manages permitted patient/examination records and generates Patient Dose Summaries.
- **Management viewer:** reviews permitted group-level analytics and reports.

No patient login or patient portal is included in the MVP. Patient Dose Summary is generated and exported by authorized institutional staff.

## 5. Functional scope

### Included in MVP
- Staff login and server-side role/institution authorization.
- Manual upload of CT RDSR files.
- File validation, processing status, parser proof of concept and safe failure handling.
- Patient and examination records with institution-aware matching/review.
- Irradiation-event storage and examination details.
- Patient search, profile and chronological examination history.
- Event-level CTDIvol/DLP display and acquisition parameters when available.
- Eligible Effective Dose estimate with method and coefficient provenance.
- Dashboard totals, recent activity and eligible group-level DRL exceedance summary.
- DRL reference selection and compatible group comparison.
- Reports Configuration, Preview/Review, PDF export and report history.
- Basic traceability and audit metadata.

### MVP report types
| Code | Audience | Scope | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EXAMINATION_DOSE` | Medical Physicist / authorized professional | One examination | Technical dose and source-data report |
| `PATIENT_DOSE_SUMMARY` | Patient-facing informational output | Multiple examinations for one patient | Plain-language longitudinal summary generated by authorized staff |
| `DOSE_ANALYTICS_DRL` | Medical Physicist / Management | Compatible group of examinations | Group-level dose statistics and DRL comparison |

### Main navigation
1. Dashboard
2. Upload / Sync
3. Patients & Exams
4. DRL Comparison
5. Reports

Patient Profile, Examination Details, Reports Configuration and Report Preview/Review are supporting views/workflows. Login is separate. “Sync” in the navigation does not imply automatic PACS/scanner integration; MVP ingestion remains manual.

## 6. Out of scope / deferred

- PACS connectivity, DICOM networking and scanner auto-sync.
- Patient self-registration, patient portal or patient account access.
- Real-time alerting or automated clinical escalation.
- Diagnosis, treatment recommendations or patient-specific risk prediction.
- Direct organ-dose calculation or advanced Monte Carlo modelling.
- Automatic merging of ambiguous patient identities.
- Multi-modality exposure tracking beyond the CT scope.
- Production clinical deployment, regulatory certification or hospital-wide integration.
- Advanced department/scanner performance suite beyond the agreed DRL group analysis.

## 7. Scientific and data boundaries

- CTDIvol is a standardized scanner output index, not direct individual absorbed dose.
- Never sum CTDIvol across events/examinations and label it cumulative patient dose.
- Preserve irradiation-event measurements and original units.
- Cumulative DLP is allowed only for eligible non-duplicated event values with compatible units and documented inclusion rules.
- Estimated Effective Dose = DLP × an applicable region/category-specific coefficient only when validated. It is an estimate, not direct measurement or individual risk.
- Do not apply adult coefficients to pediatric cases.
- Missing or unsupported values remain NULL and display as “Not Available”, never zero.
- DRLs are optimization benchmarks for representative groups, not individual patient limits. Do not show patient-level pass/fail.
- DRL comparisons require compatible metric, unit, body region/protocol and patient category as applicable; display N.
- Do not use Effective Dose as a standard CT DRL metric.

## 8. Data and privacy assumptions

The team currently has no confirmed real-patient dataset. Development and demonstration must use synthetic or authorized de-identified research RDSR files. The original DICOM file should be stored outside the public repository in access-controlled storage; the database should retain secure references and hashes. No identifiable patient data, secrets, credentials or raw clinical files may be committed to GitHub.

## 9. Success criteria

The project succeeds as an MVP when the team can demonstrate a supported RDSR upload through extraction and patient association, view the resulting longitudinal history, show event-level indicators with correct missing-data handling, calculate only eligible estimates, perform a scientifically matched group-level DRL comparison when data supports it, and generate/export all three report types with correct scope validation and provenance.

## 10. Limitations

RADSAFE is an academic/research prototype, not a certified medical device or a replacement for clinical judgement. It must not be used as the sole basis for patient-care decisions. Any future clinical use would require independent technical/scientific validation, institutional approval, privacy/security assessment and applicable regulatory review.
